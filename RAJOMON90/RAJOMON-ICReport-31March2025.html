Our reporting engine has been built on our house view asset classes and benchmarks. These largely align with your asset classes other than Fixed Interest & Credit, whereby we utilised Duration and Floating categories. Our Duration and your Fixed Interest categories align, whereby their primarily driver of return is interest rate sensitivity, with generally high-grade government issuance making up the majority of exposure.

Our credit exposures are generally floating rate in nature - so we separately categorise. We note that Linda previously requested 'Credit' to be categorised as a Growth asset when the allocation was created. As noted, traditional IG and HY credit exposures we would typically categorise as defensive assets. Private Credit exposures that are asset based lending (as is common with Australian Private Credit) we prefer to categorise as Real Assets, given the primary underlying asset is property in nature. Unlisted US Private Credit that is largely corporate lending in nature, we allocate as defensive fixed interest exposures. However use of Listed Private Credit assets, such as LEND, we would prefer are categorised as an Alternative given the sentiment heavy linkage in points of crisis leaving defensive characteristics limited.

Turning to your Alternatives categorisation. The below analysis shows a relatively high level of correlation and sensitivity of your Alternative portfolio to equity markets. Given equities are already the largest risk allocation category within the portfolio, our existing Alternative portfolio is unlikely to provide much diversification of outcome in points of market stress. We think there is an opportunity to reallocate the current Alternative exposures that are Equity industry sub-index ETFs linked to the Health & Wealth mandate of the strategy - and imbed them as core and continuing allocations within your Australian and Global Equity allocations. We believe this would better show the intent of Health & Wealth being part of the DNA of our equity allocation. This would then free up allocation within our Alternative category to seek investments with return profiles differentiated from equity markets.

Our team has separately prepared an initial asset allocation review. We would welcome a discussion at the next Investment Committee focused around the intent and return profiles sought from Alternative, Credit and Fixed interest allocations. Per above we have our opinion, however these are a major portfolio governance settings that need agreement of the IC. Once agreed we can then finalise top-down allocation category mapping, align our reporting engine naming conventions and benchmarks accordingly, and then go about providing recommendations for any restructuring of ETFs within asset classes.

MARKET OVERVIEW - March 2025

  • Australian share market took a lead from the US share market and dropped -3.3% in March on fears of a global trade war and intensifying fears of recession. Recent events highlight the challenges our economy faces, namely our reliance on exports to China. Australia sends around 35% of its iron ore to China.
  • The Trump administration scheduled to announce at the beginning of April a raft of new reciprocal tariffs on trading partners worldwide. The size and extent of those tariffs remained unknown, and that kind of uncertainty did zero to boost investor sentiment.
  • Markets interpreted the expected new tariffs as the catalyst to crimp global economic growth and potentially reignite inflation, leading to fewer than originally expected interest rate cuts over CY 2025.
  • More recent views (post March 2025) are that after the dust settles, effective tariffs on US imports could settle around 15% next year, compared to figures currently pushed out by the Trump administration, 15% doesn't seem that high, but still the highest in almost a century.
  • Global market fell -5.0% in the month March led by the US S&P 500 Index down -6.2%. The tech-heavy NASDAQ benchmark was down -8.2% for the month. European markets fared slightly better, benefiting from ongoing rotation from investors seeking alternatives to the US. The STOXX Europe 600 benchmark was down -3.7%.
  • The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) has previously noted that the enduring strength in employment was a potential roadblock to further rate cuts. In the US, the Federal Reserve maintained rates at the current level with minimal changes to the dot-plot projections. The US dollar continued to weaken against major currencies.
  • The monthly CPI indicator rose +2.4% in the 12 months to February. Largest contributors to the annual movement were Food and non-alcoholic beverages (+3.1%), Alcohol and tobacco (+6.7%), and Housing (+1.8%).
  • Bond yields rose slightly in March, the Australian 10-year bond rate was up 13 bps to 4.42% bps. Whilst US 10-year bond edged up 1 bps to 4.21% but was higher mid-month.
  • Gold continued to hit record highs with the yellow metal topping US$3000 for the first time and closing about US$3100.
